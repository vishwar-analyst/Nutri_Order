# General Health Considerations for Food Recommendations

NutriMatch is a restaurant menu discovery assistant, not a medical assessment service. It must not diagnose, promise disease control, prescribe diet/medication, or advise medication changes. Health selections are optional and are not persisted. Present general menu facts and missing data, then direct disease-specific choices to a qualified healthcare professional.

For diabetes, prediabetes, and elevated blood glucose: show known total carbohydrate and serving context; consider refined grains, added sugar, fibre, protein, sides, and portions. Do not call a traditional, millet, ragi, or brown-rice dish blood-glucose safe. Individual response varies.

For high blood pressure or low-sodium preferences: sodium is unknown unless measured. Salt, sauces, seasoning, and preparation vary. Never call an item low-sodium without verified data. Do not recommend potassium-rich foods or salt substitutes as treatment. For low blood pressure, do not automatically recommend salt or extra fluids; the cause and care plan matter.

For heart disease, high cholesterol, or triglycerides: consider sodium, saturated/trans fat, frying, and cooking fats only when documented. Total fat alone is insufficient. For kidney disease, nutrient targets are individualized; recommend clinician or renal dietitian guidance rather than ranking foods.

Status labels describe evidence limits: `generally_suitable`, `portion_control`, `preparation_matters`, `check_ingredients`, `limited_information`, `seek_professional_advice`. Never use an absolute medical safety label.

## Millet batters and staples

Ragi, kambu (pearl millet), thinai (foxtail millet), varagu (kodo millet), and kuthiraivali (barnyard millet) may be soaked and ground with urad dal or combined with flour and water. Dosa is cooked on a griddle; idli batter is steamed; fermentation, rice content, size, oil, and side dishes vary. A millet name does not mean low carbohydrate or a guaranteed health benefit.

Pongal commonly cooks grain with moong dal and seasons with pepper, cumin, ginger, curry leaves, and tempering. Ghee, oil, cashew, salt, and grain-to-dal proportions vary. Adai uses rice and a mixture of dals, typically made into a thicker griddle cake. Pesarattu is commonly made from soaked whole green gram batter. Kuzhi paniyaram uses batter cooked in a mould; oil use varies.

Kambu koozh and ragi kali are millet-and-water porridge forms. Koozh may be served fermented or diluted, sometimes with buttermilk, onion, and salt. Ragi kali is typically cooked to a firm consistency. These names do not establish exact ingredients, serving size, or nutrition.

## Rice, curries, and sides

Sambar rice combines cooked rice with dal-and-vegetable sambar. Rasam rice pairs rice with a thinner seasoned broth. Lemon rice and puliyodarai are seasoned rice preparations; nuts, sesame, oil, tamarind, and salt use vary. Keerai masiyal is cooked greens mashed or blended and served with rice. Kootu combines vegetables and dal; poriyal is a cooked vegetable side; avial combines vegetables with a coconut-based seasoning and may include curd. Poondu and vatha kuzhambu are tamarind-based gravies.

Sundal starts with a legume (chickpea, green gram, cowpea, or peanut) that is soaked/boiled and seasoned; coconut, salt, and tempering are optional. Kozhukattai uses rice-flour dough and may have sweet or savoury filling; pidi kozhukattai is hand-shaped and steamed. Ask staff about exact fillings and cooking ingredients.

## Drinks and animal-protein recipes

Neer mor and spiced buttermilk are diluted seasoned curd/yoghurt drinks and contain dairy unless the actual preparation differs and is confirmed. Panagam often combines water, jaggery, and spices; sweetness varies. Tender coconut water should be confirmed as fresh and unsweetened if that matters.

Fish curry/kulambu simmers fish in a regional tamarind, tomato, spice, and sometimes coconut gravy. Country chicken kulambu and pepper chicken use poultry with spices and vary in oil and gravy. Grilled chicken may contain a marinade and uses shared cooking surfaces. Egg dosa cooks egg with dosa batter; egg curry simmers boiled egg in gravy. Fish fry may be pan- or deep-fried. Always confirm species, marinade, oil, salt, dairy, nuts, and cross-contact with the restaurant.
